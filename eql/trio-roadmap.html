Bard-Primary Trio Roadmap

A no-overlap partition of all 16 classes into Bard-led trios, ranked by how closely each one reproduces the self-sufficiency of the first deep-dive (tank + healer + Bard support). The Constraint and the Math

16 classes total, Bard fixed as the constant thread. That leaves 15 other classes to pair up two at a time (Secondary + Tertiary), each used exactly once across the whole roadmap. 15 is odd, so this partitions into 7 trios using 14 classes, with 1 class left over that has no home in a clean no-overlap partition.

Ranking Logic

Trio 1's shape — a hybrid tank (Shadow Knight) paired with a Wisdom-caster healer (Druid) — is the strongest self-sufficient pattern EQL's stance/invocation system supports: a real tanking stance, a real healing invocation (Divine), and Bard's free buff songs on top. Every subsequent trio is ranked by how closely it can reproduce that self-sufficiency once the ingredients for it (dedicated tanks, dedicated priests) run out, using three confirmed mechanical levers:

  1. Tank stance access — Defensive and Mage Hunter stances are limited to PAL/SHD/WAR/BER (WAR gets Defensive but not Mage Hunter; BER gets Mage Hunter but not Defensive). Once a class with real mitigation is gone, the trio's durability drops.
  2. Divine invocation access — heals-while-casting is available to BST, CLR, DRU, PAL, RNG, SHM. Once these are exhausted, no trio has passive healing at all.
  3. Category-stacking bonuses — three invocations explicitly scale with how many of a given category (Intelligence: ENC/MAG/NEC/WIZ; Wisdom: CLR/DRU/SHM; non-hybrid caster: CLR/DRU/ENC/MAG/NEC/SHM/WIZ) are present in your trio. Arcane Mastery (cast speed/mana cost) and Empower/Over Channel (damage/healing/resist penetration) all get stronger with a second class from the same category. Since Bard never counts toward these categories, the only way to trigger the bonus is to pair two same-category classes as Secondary+Tertiary — which becomes the best available lever once tanks and priests are gone.

Trio 2 · "The good-aligned mirror"
Bard / Paladin / Cleric Planned
Tank + healer
Reproduces trio 1's shape almost exactly. Paladin has the same stance kit as Shadow Knight (Balanced, Defensive, Mage Hunter, Offensive) plus Divine invocation, which Shadow Knight lacks — this trio can passively off-heal through Paladin alone even before Cleric casts anything. Cleric is the strongest dedicated healer in the game and shares Bard's Inversion/Over Channel/Recovery pool. The most obvious "next best" — same shape, arguably stronger healing access than trio 1, at the cost of Druid's travel/root utility.
Trio 3 · "The raw tank"
Bard / Warrior / Shaman Planned
Tank + healer
Uses up the last pure-tank class and the last Wisdom priest. Warrior has the widest pure-melee stance kit (Balanced, Defensive, Offensive, Striker) but only one invocation (Unyielding — 2x health regen, +25% fear/mez/charm resist, no mana cost), so this trio trades Divine/off-healing for Warrior's superior raw mitigation and Striker's burst damage. Shaman covers the healing gap directly. Ranked third rather than second because Warrior's invocation pool is thinner than Paladin's (no Divine access at all) — this trio leans harder on Shaman to do all the healing rather than sharing the load.
Trio 4 · "The healing-hybrid pair"
Bard / Ranger / Beastlord Planned
Hybrid + hybrid
This is where the priest well runs dry (Cleric and Shaman both used), so the roadmap pivots to the best remaining substitute: Ranger and Beastlord are both Hybrid classes that individually carry Divine invocation — meaning this trio keeps passive off-healing alive without a dedicated priest at all. Beastlord's pet adds a second tanking body (echoing Shadow Knight's pet from trio 1), and both classes get Spellblade for weaving a spell into melee swings. No Wisdom-category stacking bonus applies (Ranger/Beastlord are Hybrid, not Wisdom), but having Divine represented twice keeps the option resilient if one class's build leans elsewhere.
Trio 5 · "The stacked casters, burst"
Bard / Wizard / Necromancer Planned
Stacked Intelligence casters
First trio to deliberately exploit the category-stacking mechanic: Wizard and Necromancer are both Intelligence classes and both "non-hybrid casters," so this trio gets the bonus tier of Arcane Mastery, Empower, and Over Channel simultaneously — a real, confirmed mechanical bonus, not a synthesis guess. Necromancer's lifetap/pet line gives some self-sustain in place of a dedicated tank, and Wizard shares Bard's Inviolable invocation (uninterruptible casting for both). No healing access at all — a glass-cannon trio, best suited to grouped or raid content rather than solo leveling.
Trio 6 · "The stacked casters, control"
Bard / Magician / Enchanter Planned
Stacked Intelligence casters
The other Intelligence-stacking pair, using the same Arcane Mastery/Empower/Over Channel bonus tier as trio 5. Magician's pet substitutes for a tank the same way Necromancer's does. Enchanter brings its own charm/mez kit, which stacks with Bard's own Lull/Mez songs for a crowd-control-heavy trio — good for tough single-pull adds, weaker for sustained group damage than trio 5. Ranked just behind Wizard/Necromancer because Enchanter/Magician's damage output is generally more utility-oriented than Wizard's burst nukes or Necromancer's DoT/lifetap sustain.
Trio 7 · "What's left"
Bard / Berserker / Monk Planned
Pure melee
The last two pure-melee classes (Rogue is the odd one out — see below). Berserker is the only remaining class with Mage Hunter stance, giving this trio some anti-caster mitigation despite having no dedicated tank archetype; Monk contributes Evasive stance and strong single-target melee DPS. Neither class carries any invocation beyond Unyielding, so this trio has zero healing or caster utility of any kind — the weakest self-sufficiency profile in the roadmap, best suited to raid/group content where healing comes from outside the trio.
Leftover: Rogue. 13 is odd, so one class doesn't get a clean partition slot. Rogue was set aside rather than Monk or Berserker because its kit (poison application, evasion, positional backstab) overlaps most heavily with the other pure-melee classes already placed in trio 7 — losing it costs the roadmap the least mechanical diversity. That's a judgment call, not a mechanical fact: if you'd rather see Rogue in the lineup, the cleanest swap is dropping Berserker or Monk from trio 7 and slotting Rogue in instead, or breaking trio 7 into a genuinely melee-only "leftover pair" question once trios 2-6 have played out.
