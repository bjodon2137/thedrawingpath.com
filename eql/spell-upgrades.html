EverQuest Legends · Spell Upgrade System

What Happens When a Spell Ranks Up

Every scribed spell starts at Tier 0 and can be pushed to Tier 10 by feeding it motes. This is a compiled reference for exactly what each tier changes, by spell category — plus a calculator to plug in any spell's base numbers and see it at any rank.

⚠ This is a beta/launch-era system and still being tuned by the devs. The rates below are fitted from in-game upgrade-window readings and combat logs (per eqltools.com), not official published numbers — treat exact percentages as "best current estimate," especially for anything not yet verified past rank 8.

01 The Basics

How upgrading actually works, mechanically.

Tier range: 0–10

Every spell starts at Tier 0. Feed it motes via the spell's info window (Alt+Left-Click or hold Right-Click on the icon in your spell bar, spellbook, or Actions window) to raise it. Past Tier 0 the spell's display name gets a Roman numeral suffix — Minor Healing becomes Minor Healing II, III, etc.

Mote cost doubles every tier

Reaching rank 1 costs 2 motes' worth of experience, rank 2 costs 4, rank 8 costs 128, and rank 10 costs 1,024 cumulative. Higher-grade motes are worth more experience per mote, but the doubling curve means Tier 8–10 is a serious investment either way.

Only the stat block changes

Spell description/lore text stays exactly the same at every tier — don't expect flavor text to update, only the numeric stats in the tooltip.

Motes are untradable

As of the July 14 patch, every mote must be spent on the character that earned it — no auction house, no trading between your own alts.

Void-touched Potential

A capped weekly token (up to 3/week from raid voidling activities) that merges like a mote but instantly grants one full rank to any item or spell — the efficient way to close out the expensive top tiers.

Summon-item spells (e.g. Summoned Dagger) hand you gear at the spell's own tier — a Tier 10 summon spell produces a +10 item, if that item supports upgrades.

05 Crowd Control — Level Cap, Not Stat Cut

Mez, charm, fear, stun, and pacify don't get cheaper/faster from tiering — they get more reliable against higher-level targets.

What actually moves

Each rank raises the highest NPC level the spell can successfully land on. This shipped for Mez and Charm first, then was extended to Pacify, Fear, and Stun.

Base ceilings (Tier 0)

Charm reads 25, Mesmerize reads 55, Lull reads 30 as their base level ceiling — check each spell's own description for its number, since these vary per spell.

Rate per tier: unconfirmed

The patch notes state the rule but not the exact rate — how many levels one rank actually adds to the ceiling hasn't been pinned down yet. Treat this as "gets better with tier" rather than a known formula.

06 What Doesn't Change

Things a rank never touches, regardless of spell type.

Instant & Permanent duration spells

No duration bonus applies — there's nothing to extend.

Zero-mana spells

Nothing on the mana line to reduce (e.g. Cannibalize-style self-cost spells).

Buff/debuff magnitude

A rank shortens cast/recovery/mana and extends duration, but the actual value a buff grants — a damage shield's absorb amount, a stat buff's numbers — stays fixed at every rank.

Innate weapon procs — odd ranks

Procs only gain a rank every two spell ranks, each worth +6% of base. Odd-numbered ranks don't move them.

DoT-tick & heal magnitude

Not exposed in the upgrade window at all, and not cleanly separable from focus effects and crit scaling in the combat log — currently unresolved, unlike direct nuke damage which reads cleanly.
