Hunting Grounds by Class

Some classes get direct-damage spells that only work against a specific creature category — Undead or Construct/Elemental ("Summoned"). Parking one of those classes in a zone dominated by its bonus-damage type turns every fight into a mana-efficient, fast-killing loop. This page maps which classes get which type, and which zones actually have the concentration of that type to make it worth leveling there.

2 confirmed type-restricted categories: Undead, Construct/Elemental

The Mechanic

eqlwiki.com's spell pages carry an explicit Target Type field. Most direct-damage spells are Target Type: Single (works on anything). A handful are restricted to a specific creature category — cast them on the wrong target and they simply don't land. Two such categories are confirmed and itemized below: Undead and Construct/Elemental (the community and even the wiki itself casually call the second one "Summoned" — see the callout on The Hole below for a direct quote using that exact term).

Both confirmed lines follow the same shape: a low-level "Ward" spell, then one or more higher-level upgrades that do more damage for more mana, learned at different levels by different classes. Because these spells cost the same mana as a generic nuke but hit harder against their target type, camping a zone that's actually dominated by that type turns your class's normal kill speed into something meaningfully faster — this is the "hunting ground" effect.

Undead Specialists Target Type: Undead

Who Gets It

Necromancer

Ward Undead (L6) → Expulse Undead (L19) → Dismiss Undead (L28) → Expel Undead (L38) → Banish Undead (L46)

The longest confirmed Undead chain in the game — five tiers spanning the whole leveling curve. The top three tiers (Dismiss/Expel/Banish Undead) are read off the Necromancer class page directly; unlike Ward Undead and Expulse Undead they don't yet have independently-confirmed standalone spell pages, so treat those three as "sourced, not double-verified."

Paladin

Ward Undead (L1, autogranted) → Expulse Undead (L30)

Starts with Ward Undead for free at level 1 — no other class gets it this early. A long gap to the L30 upgrade, but the early access makes Undead zones a strong low-level Paladin option.

Cleric

Ward Undead (L4) → Expulse Undead (L13)

Both tiers land early, well ahead of Paladin's upgrade and well ahead of Shadow Knight's whole line. Also the only class with dual specialization — see the Summoned section below.

Shadow Knight

Ward Undead (L18) → Expulse Undead (L39)

Latest-arriving of the four Undead classes. Thematically odd (Shadow Knights lean undead-aligned) but mechanically it's the weakest access window of the group.

Summoned Specialists Target Type: Construct/Elemental

Who Gets It

Druid

Ward Summoned (L2) → Expulse Summoned (L13)

Earliest access of any class — Ward Summoned lands at level 2. Matches the framing in the Bard/Shadow Knight/Druid trio guide: Druid's kit front-loads utility, and this is one more piece of it.

Cleric

Ward Summoned (L17)

No confirmed upgrade tier yet — just the base spell. Combined with the Undead line above, Cleric is the only class confirmed to have both type-restricted damage categories, making it the most zone-flexible of the six specialist classes.

Magician

Ward Summoned (L9)

Thematically fitting — Magicians summon their own pets — though the base spell is all that's confirmed so far, no upgrade tier found.

Ranger

Ward Summoned (L16) → Dismiss Summoned (L33)

Second-longest confirmed Summoned chain after Druid's.

Where to Go

The Hole is the single best answer here — confirmed almost word-for-word on eqlwiki.com: "Many of the monsters in the Hole fit the category of 'summoned' creatures... classes that get the line of spells that affect summoned creatures... will be significantly more effective." This directly validates it as the top pick, not just a flavor guess.

Classes With No Type-Restricted Bonus

Checked directly against eqlwiki.com class and spell pages — these ten classes have no confirmed Undead- or Construct/Elemental-restricted damage spell. For these classes, zone choice should follow normal leveling-efficiency logic (loot, XP rate, group availability) rather than chasing a mob-type bonus that doesn't exist.

Warrior Rogue Monk Berserker Beastlord Shaman Wizard Enchanter Bard

Notes on a few near-misses that don't count: Rogue's Assassinate has a Humanoid-only restriction on eqlwiki.com but is explicitly labeled "Not currently implemented in EQ Legends." Wizard has no Giant/Dragon-restricted nuke despite the lore fit — checked the full L1–50 spell list directly. Shaman's only Animal-tagged spells are non-damage utility (Sense Animals, Charm Animals) — no restricted nuke.

Caveats

Necromancer's "Animal/Plant" claim is flagged, not confirmed. The Necromancer class page on eqlwiki.com states the class is "classified as anti-natural" with "unique spells to attack animals and plant based mobs" — but no direct-damage spell with an Animal or Plant Target Type was found anywhere in the Necromancer's spell tables during this research pass. This looks like it may be lore/flavor text not (yet) backed by an actual implemented spell, or the spell exists under a name not yet located. Don't route a Necromancer to an Animal- or Plant-heavy zone expecting a confirmed bonus — only the five-tier Undead chain above is verified for this class.
Mistmoore Castle's undead typing is unconfirmed. Mayong Mistmoore's vampire stronghold is dominated by "Ancille," Gargoyle, Gypsy, and Familiar-named mobs — no explicit Undead Creature Type tag was found on eqlwiki.com for these, unlike the Plane of Hate/Fear tables which do carry that tagging. The vampire flavor is strong, but flavor text isn't the same as a confirmed mob type — treat this zone as unconfirmed for Undead-specialist leveling until someone checks the individual NPC pages directly.
The Hole and Kithicor Forest aren't in the Loot Codex yet. Both are referenced above with real, sourced level ranges and named mobs, but neither has full named-mob/loot itemization on this site the way the other 32 zones do. Worth a dedicated research pass if you plan to actually camp either one.
